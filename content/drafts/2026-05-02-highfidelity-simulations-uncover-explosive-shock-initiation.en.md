This paper details a high-fidelity mesoscale computational framework for simulating shock initiation in plastic-bonded explosives (PBXs) due to flyer plate impact. The approach focuses on interface-resolved reactive calculations, employing higher-order (fifth-order WENO) schemes for accurate shock and interfacial dynamics, along with sharp-interface treatments for material interactions. The simulations incorporate atomistics-consistent material models for HMX, with grid resolution down to the atomistic scale (O(nm)), and crystal geometries derived from nano-CT imaging of experiments. The framework tracks the impacting flyer plate, energetic crystal, and binder as distinct phases, capturing flyer deformation and relief wave effects.

The study finds that boundary conditions for flyer impact significantly influence the resulting shock wave and hotspot characteristics, which are not accurately reproduced by simpler phenomenological boundary conditions. Another finding is the importance of the atomistics-consistent material model for HMX, combined with a temperature-dependent specific heat relation, in accurately capturing the material's elastoplastic response. This model demonstrates consistency with molecular dynamics simulations by resolving shear dislocations under weak shock conditions and aligns with experimental data for hotspot temperatures during strong shock initiation.

While this work represents a significant advancement in the field of energetic materials and shock-to-detonation transition modeling, its direct relevance to the prediction of aerodynamic heating on high-speed missiles is limited. The physics explored pertains to internal material response under extreme shock loading rather than external aerothermodynamic phenomena. However, the high-fidelity computational methods and the rigorous validation against atomistic and experimental data could inform general practices in high-fidelity CFD.
