Recent research focuses on characterizing and modeling shock-wave/boundary-layer interactions (SWBLI) relevant to hypersonic vehicle design. Several studies at Mach 2.5 in circular test sections using cone-cylinder centerbodies provide benchmark-quality datasets for CFD validation, avoiding corner effects present in rectangular configurations. These experiments, detailed in multiple NASA reports, employ surface flow visualization, pressure measurements, hot-wire anemometry, and PIV to analyze unseparated, incipiently separated, and fully separated interactions, including 3D effects induced by offsetting the centerbody. Complementary numerical simulations using RANS models (SST k-ω and Spalart-Allmaras) assess the accuracy of predicting axisymmetric SWBLI, revealing discrepancies in separation characteristics. Furthermore, a method for extracting high-resolution skin-friction fields from pressure-sensitive paint (PSP) data in SWBLI is presented, based on a coupling relation between skin friction and surface pressure, validated against surface-stress-sensitive film data. Finally, fundamental aerodynamics of slender bodies, missile aerodynamics, tactical missile aerodynamics, supersonic missile aerodynamic heating, and missile airframe/exhaust plume interactions are examined in several reports.
