This paper investigates secondary instabilities that develop from the primary global mode within an incident shock-wave boundary-layer interaction (SWBLI) at Mach 2.15. The authors combine direct numerical simulations (DNS) with three-dimensional global stability analysis (3-D GSA) to characterize the evolution and properties of these secondary instability branches. They specifically examine the influence of spanwise domain width, considering both a fundamental wavelength (λm) and twice that wavelength (2λm).

In the λm domain, the study identifies a time-periodic unsteadiness emerging as a secondary instability after the primary global mode saturates, which is classified as a supercritical Hopf bifurcation. When the domain width is doubled to 2λm, an additional secondary growth is observed, accompanied by a wavelength doubling phenomenon. This is attributed to a stationary secondary instability with a wavelength of 2λm, identified as a subcritical pitchfork bifurcation. The DNS and 3-D GSA results show consistent interpretations of these secondary instability scenarios, highlighting the sensitivity of the flow dynamics to the spanwise domain size.

Understanding these complex instability mechanisms in SWBLI is crucial for predicting and mitigating flow unsteadiness and associated loads on high-speed vehicles. While this work does not directly address aerodynamic heating or surrogate modeling, the fundamental insights into high-speed flow physics are relevant to the broader aerothermodynamics community, particularly for missile design where SWBLI can significantly impact performance and thermal loads.
