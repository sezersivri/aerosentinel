This paper investigates the conversion of incident acoustic energy into vortical motion and viscous dissipation as a plane wave passes through large-aspect-ratio slit geometries. The authors conduct direct numerical simulations across a wide parameter space, varying incident sound pressure level (ISPL), Strouhal number (St), and Reynolds number (Re). Spectral proper orthogonal decomposition is employed to identify energy-ranked coherent structures at different frequencies, allowing for the construction of mode-by-mode fields for spectral kinetic energy (KE) and viscous loss (VL) to analyze the acoustic absorption mechanisms.

The study reveals that at an ISPL of 150 dB, the acoustic–hydrodynamic energy conversion is highest when St is less than or equal to four times a reference Strouhal number, corresponding to an effective Keulegan–Carpenter number greater than 40. In this regime, three-dimensional simulations indicate that the dominant flow response is two-dimensional, characterized by the oscillatory shear layer near the slit corners rolling up into shedding vortices. Viscous loss accounts for 20% to 60% of the kinetic energy contribution. For larger Strouhal numbers, the confinement of the Stokes layer leads to X-shaped near-slit modes, which reduces the energy input by approximately 50%. The influence of Reynolds number is also discussed.

This work provides fundamental insights into the fluid dynamics of acoustic energy dissipation in confined geometries, detailing the interplay between acoustic waves, vortex dynamics, and viscous effects. While not directly related to high-speed aerodynamic heating, it contributes to the broader understanding of energy transfer in fluid systems.
