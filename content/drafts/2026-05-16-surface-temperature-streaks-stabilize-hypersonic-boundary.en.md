Boscagli and co-authors investigate a passive control method to stabilize the second Mack mode instability in hypersonic boundary layers, aiming to suppress laminar-to-turbulent transition. Their approach involves generating steady streaks within the boundary layer by applying a spanwise non-uniform surface temperature distribution. This method capitalizes on the aerothermodynamic characteristics of the flow. The study employs direct numerical simulations (DNS) of a hypersonic boundary layer over a flat plate, covering Mach numbers from 4.8 to 6 and wall temperature ratios relevant to both wind tunnel and flight conditions.

The computational analyses demonstrate that this control method can reduce the energy of the second Mack mode by up to approximately 60%. The effectiveness is strongly dependent on the streak wavelength parameter. For a Mach 6 configuration, the most linearly amplified second Mack mode disturbance frequency showed nearly optimum performance when the spanwise wavelength was approximately 8–10 times the local boundary layer thickness.

These findings offer a promising direction for controlling hypersonic boundary layers, which is critical for managing the extreme heat fluxes experienced by high-speed vehicles. The work provides valuable guidance for future experimental campaigns designed to validate this control strategy, potentially leading to more efficient thermal management systems for hypersonic missiles.
