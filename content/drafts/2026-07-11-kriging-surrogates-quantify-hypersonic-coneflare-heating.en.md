El Khoury and Hickey developed a non-intrusive uncertainty quantification (UQ) framework to assess how aleatory variability in freestream conditions, wall temperature, transport parameters, and nose bluntness affects design-relevant quantities of interest for hypersonic cone-flare configurations. Their approach couples the SU2 RANS solver, configured with the SST turbulence model, with DAKOTA for UQ. They used Latin Hypercube Sampling to generate an initial 238 RANS simulations, which were then adaptively augmented to a total of 269 simulations, covering a seven-dimensional input space.

From each simulation, six quantities of interest, including thermal loads and separation behavior, were extracted. These data points were then used to train a Kriging surrogate model. Following validation, the surrogate enabled 75,000 Monte Carlo evaluations, efficiently yielding probability distributions and Sobol global sensitivity indices for the quantities of interest. The analysis revealed that boundary-layer thermodynamics significantly influence both thermal loads and flow separation characteristics on the cone-flare geometry.

This work provides a clear, reproducible surrogate-based UQ workflow for RANS simulations of hypersonic cone-flares. The findings are valuable for understanding the sensitivity of aerothermodynamic predictions to input uncertainties, which is crucial for robust design and analysis of high-speed missiles. The demonstrated methodology also sets a foundation for future investigations into epistemic model-form and numerical uncertainties.
