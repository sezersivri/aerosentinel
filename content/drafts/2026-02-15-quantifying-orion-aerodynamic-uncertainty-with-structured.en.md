This paper introduces Structured Covariance Gaussian Networks (SCGNs), a new method for nonlinear regression and uncertainty quantification. The approach involves a pair of neural networks that parameterize the mean and a dense covariance function of a multivariate Gaussian process. These networks are trained together to maximize the log-likelihood of the observed data, with the covariance matrix constructed to ensure positive definiteness at every input. The authors also present a sampling technique to generate viable surrogate function realizations from the Gaussian process, applying the SCGN model to learn an aerodynamic response surface with built-in uncertainty for the Orion crew module.

The results indicate that SCGN provides an efficient and systematic way to learn complex nonlinear functional relationships and dense covariances. When compared to a baseline Gaussian process regressor, SCGN delivers comparable uncertainty descriptions while demonstrating improved scalability with increasing dataset size. The sample functions produced by SCGN are quick to evaluate online, making them suitable for integration into trajectory simulations.

These findings suggest that SCGN is a viable computational method for aerodynamic uncertainty quantification. Its ability to combine the flexibility of neural networks with the uncertainty quantification capabilities of Gaussian processes, particularly with improved scalability, holds promise for the design and analysis of high-speed vehicles where accurate aerodynamic predictions and their associated uncertainties are critical.
