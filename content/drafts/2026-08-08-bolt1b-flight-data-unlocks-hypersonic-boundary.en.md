This paper investigates the boundary layer transition process on the BOLT-1B flight experiment under hypersonic conditions. The authors analyze a high-quality dataset of surface pressure and heating measurements from the flight, which captured the onset of transition. To interpret these observations, they employ a computational approach combining high-fidelity baseflow solutions, stochastically forced direct numerical simulation (DNS), and plane-marching parabolized stability equation (PPSE) analysis.

The flight data revealed a marked rise in surface heating, consistent with prior BOLT-2 observations, and distinct spectral peaks in pressure data near the centerline and midspan regions at slightly higher Reynolds numbers. The forced DNS models the receptivity and nonlinear disturbance amplification within the boundary layer, supported by a modal analysis of the resulting transition. Complementary PPSE calculations provide linear growth predictions, which are then compared against the DNS results to establish a baseline consistent with linear stability theory.

Together, these methods offer a comprehensive interpretation of the instability mechanisms likely governing transition on BOLT-1B. This work provides a validated reference framework for future efforts in transition modeling within realistic hypersonic flight environments, which is crucial for accurate aerothermodynamic predictions.
