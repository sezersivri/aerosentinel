**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 75/100

This paper investigates the dissociation of high-temperature carbon dioxide, a critical process for predicting aerodynamic heating during high-speed entry into the Martian atmosphere. The authors developed a tunable diode laser absorption spectroscopy (TDLAS) system, combining CO2-TDLAS with an existing CO-TDLAS setup, to simultaneously measure temperature, CO concentration, and CO2 concentration during the dissociation process. They specifically calibrated the R(0, 88) line in the CO2 fundamental vibrational band over a temperature range of 3030–5990 K to enable these measurements.

Applying their new experimental setup, the researchers studied CO2 dissociation behaviors and identified significant discrepancies between their measurements and existing model predictions. Through sensitivity analysis, they pinpointed the key reaction impacting CO2 dissociation and subsequently updated the reaction rate coefficient for CO2 + M <=> CO + O + M using an Arrhenius equation, valid over 2980–6030 K. This revised mechanism, incorporating the updated rate coefficient, demonstrated improved accuracy in predicting CO2 dissociation behaviors across the 3000–6000 K temperature range.

This work offers a functional methodology for accurately determining the rate coefficient of specific elementary reactions over broad temperature ranges with reduced uncertainty. Such precise characterization of nonequilibrium chemical processes is fundamental for developing more accurate aerothermodynamic models, particularly for spacecraft design and heat transfer predictions in high-enthalpy Martian atmospheric entry scenarios.
