**Type:** 💻 Numerical/CFD | **Relevance:** 75/100

Cogo and co-authors investigate compressible turbulent boundary layers over prism-shaped roughness using direct numerical simulations (DNS) at Mach 2 and 4. They explore the influence of compressibility and wall thermal conditions (adiabatic and isothermal) on the boundary layer structure and the validity of the Reynolds analogy. The study aims to understand momentum-energy coupling in rough-wall high-speed flows and develop a wall model for predicting surface quantities.

The DNS results show that while roughness disrupts the near-wall momentum-energy coupling within the roughness sublayer, the Reynolds analogy recovers in the outer region, exhibiting smooth-wall-like similarity for velocity and temperature fields. A systematic comparison of different Reynolds analogy formulations indicates that a mixed Prandtl number approach provides the most accurate heat-flux predictions among those tested. Based on these observations, they developed a modular wall model that couples drag prediction with a compressibility transformation and a temperature-velocity closure. This model predicts both wall-shear stress and heat flux using only roughness geometry and information from the matching location, demonstrating good a priori agreement with the DNS data.

This work contributes to a better understanding of aerothermodynamics in high-speed flows over rough surfaces, which is critical for missile design where surface imperfections or ablation can occur. The developed wall model offers a practical tool for CFD simulations, potentially improving the accuracy of heat flux and drag predictions without the computational expense of fully resolving roughness in every simulation.
