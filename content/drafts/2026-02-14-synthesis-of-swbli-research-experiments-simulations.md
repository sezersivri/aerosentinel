Research on shock wave boundary layer interactions (SWBLI) continues to be a critical area, with ongoing efforts to improve both experimental data and computational models. A series of NASA reports detail experimental investigations of Mach 2.5 axisymmetric SWBLIs, specifically designed for CFD validation. These experiments focus on providing well-defined boundary conditions and detailed measurements to address the scarcity of high-quality supersonic and hypersonic validation datasets, as highlighted by Settles and Dodson. Complementing these experimental efforts, implicit large-eddy simulations (ILES) have been employed to study the turbulent kinetic energy budget within SWBLIs, resolving terms such as production, dissipation, and turbulent transport. These simulations, while requiring fine mesh resolution and long development domains for equilibrium, provide valuable insights into the dynamics of the interaction region. Furthermore, analytical studies have explored separated flows induced by SWBLIs, while comprehensive reviews offer up-to-date descriptions of the phenomena across transonic and supersonic regimes, including various solution approaches ranging from global methods to time-dependent Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes equations.
