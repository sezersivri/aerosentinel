Roseman and colleagues investigate ultraviolet (UV) radiation behind a hypersonic bow shock, comparing their simulations with data from the Bow Shock Ultraviolet (BSUV) flight experiment. They model the flow around a 10.16 cm radius sphere-cone vehicle flying at Mach 15, across altitudes from 20 to 70 km. The study employs the LeMANS computational fluid dynamics code, incorporating both the Park and Modified Marrone–Treanor (MMT) chemical kinetics models, with NASA’s NEQAIR code handling the radiation calculations.

The simulations show reasonable agreement with the experimental data. Between 30 and 60 km altitude, nitric oxide (NO) is identified as the primary source of UV radiation, while nitrogen Schumann–Runge emission becomes significant outside this range. Below 45 km, NO excited states are populated by collisional excitation and follow a Boltzmann distribution. At higher altitudes, exchange reactions become dominant, leading to a notable overpopulation of these excited states. Differences observed between the Park and MMT models are primarily attributed to their respective oxygen dissociation rates. Specifically, below 45 km, Park's higher oxygen dissociation rate results in lower temperatures and less UV radiance compared to MMT. Conversely, above 45 km, the Park model predicts greater NO formation and thus higher UV radiance. The MMT model's prediction for radiance dependence on altitude aligns well with experimental observations, whereas the Park model exhibits inconsistent agreement across different altitudes.

This work highlights the critical role of chemical kinetics models in accurately predicting the UV radiation environment within hypersonic shock layers. The findings are important for understanding the optical signatures of high-speed vehicles and for the development of optical sensors or communication systems operating in such extreme conditions.
