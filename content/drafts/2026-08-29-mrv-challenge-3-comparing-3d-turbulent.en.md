This paper reports on the third Magnetic Resonance Velocimetry (MRV) Challenge, an experimental activity focused on measuring 3D turbulent flow and passive scalar transport in a water channel. The setup involves water flowing past centrally positioned cubic obstacles, with a secondary flow injected from the bottom wall between the second and third obstacles. Five research teams conducted MRV measurements on a shared apparatus, coupling velocity measurements with either temperature or concentration measurements. The chosen flow regime and working fluids ensure that both temperature and concentration behave as passive scalars, allowing for direct comparison of their fields.

The measurements from the participating teams are explained in detail, then interpolated onto a common grid for comparison. The results are analyzed using line profiles, contour plots, and isosurfaces to assess the agreement between the different experimental approaches and the behavior of the passive scalar mixing. Specific quantitative metrics for comparison, such as RMSE or deviation percentages, are not provided in the abstract.

While this work contributes to the validation and advancement of MRV techniques for complex turbulent flows and scalar mixing, its direct relevance to high-speed missile aerothermodynamics or the application of Gaussian Process surrogate models for heating prediction is minimal. The study focuses on low-speed, incompressible, internal channel flow, which is far removed from the high-Mach number, compressible external flows encountered by missiles.
