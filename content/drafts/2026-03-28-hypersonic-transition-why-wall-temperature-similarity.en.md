**Type:** 💻 Numerical/CFD | **Relevance:** 65/100

This paper presents a numerical analysis investigating the importance of wall-to-recovery temperature ratio similarity in hypersonic boundary-layer transition. The study focuses on a cone-cylinder-flare geometry at Mach 6 and a Reynolds number of 1.2 x 10^7. The authors use global stability and resolvent analysis to compare three cases: typical flight conditions, standard wind-tunnel conditions, and a cooled-wall wind-tunnel case designed to achieve wall-to-recovery temperature similarity with flight.

The results indicate that global bubble modes are strongly destabilized by wall cooling. Critically, Mach and Reynolds number similarities alone are insufficient to replicate the linear transition mechanisms observed in hypersonic flight. In typical wind-tunnel conditions, both first and second modes contribute to transition, whereas flight conditions primarily show amplification of the second mode. The cooled-wall case, which incorporates wall-to-recovery temperature ratio similarity, successfully reproduces the linear transition mechanisms seen in flight, even at lower stagnation temperatures.

These findings highlight the crucial role of the wall-to-recovery temperature ratio in hypersonic boundary-layer transition studies. The work the challenges in accurately replicating flight conditions in ground-test campaigns, which has direct implications for validating computational tools used in aerothermodynamic design and heating prediction.
