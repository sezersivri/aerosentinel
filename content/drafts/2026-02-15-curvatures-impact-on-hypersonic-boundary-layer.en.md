The paper investigates the impact of wall curvature (flat-plate, concave, and convex) on the evolution of Mack modes within hypersonic boundary layers. It employs the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes equations to conduct a parametric study, aiming to understand how varying curvature affects boundary layer transition mechanisms.

Results indicate that convex curvature stabilizes the Mack mode due to favorable pressure gradients. As convex curvature increases, this stabilizing effect becomes more pronounced, suppressing the Mack mode compared to a flat plate. Conversely, concave curvature destabilizes the Mack mode through adverse pressure gradients, amplifying it as concave curvature intensifies.

This understanding is significant for the aerothermodynamics community, particularly in the design of hypersonic vehicles where precise control over boundary layer transition is essential for managing thermal loads and optimizing aerodynamic performance.
