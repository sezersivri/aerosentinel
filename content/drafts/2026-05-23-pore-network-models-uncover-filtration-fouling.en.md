This paper presents a model for filtration in membrane filters, focusing on the simultaneous effects of small particle adsorption and large particle sieving within a graded pore network. The approach combines continuum partial differential equations to model the transport and adsorptive fouling of small particles in each pore with a discrete Poisson arrival process and biased random walk for the sieving particles. The primary objective is to understand the relative influences and coupling of these two fouling mechanisms on the performance of filters featuring a pore-size gradient.

The authors' findings indicate that sieving qualitatively alters the rate of flux decline due to its discrete pore blockage nature. They highlight that the difference between sieving-particle sizes and the initial pore radii in each filter band plays a crucial role in determining the onset and disappearance of competition between sieving and adsorption. Furthermore, the study demonstrates a phase transition in the filter's lifetime as the arrival frequency of sieving particles increases.

While this work offers insights into complex filtration dynamics through a hybrid modeling approach, its domain is entirely separate from high-speed missile aerothermodynamics or the prediction of aerodynamic heating.
