**Type:** 🤖 ML/Aerodynamics | **Relevance:** 40/100

This paper describes a methodology for optimizing the design of a long-range Mars rotorcraft by integrating high-fidelity simulation data with machine learning techniques. The approach begins with generating extensive aerodynamic simulation databases for a hex-rotor bi-plane tailsitter aircraft across various geometric perturbations and flight conditions. Over 3,000 full aircraft aerodynamic simulations were performed using GPU-enabled OVERFLOW with an actuator disk model, taking approximately 1.5 weeks on 32 GPU nodes equipped with 128 NVIDIA V100 GPUs.

Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), sparse GPRs, and various Neural Networks were then trained as surrogate models to predict airfoil and airframe aerodynamic performance based on flight conditions, airframe geometry, and rotor control inputs. These surrogate models, combined with additional modules for aircraft mass and inertia, enabled another 3,000 aircraft simulations in CAMRAD-II in less than six hours using 240 CPU cores. This significantly reduced the computational cost for subsequent design iterations. The framework was then configured for simultaneous optimization of aircraft parameters and flight conditions, aiming for objectives such as maximum range or endurance while satisfying controllability constraints.

This work demonstrates a practical application of data-driven methods to bring higher-fidelity simulation data into the early stages of conceptual design. By creating accurate and efficient surrogate models, the design process can achieve improved accuracy and potentially identify critical design issues much earlier than traditional methods, despite the computational expense of the initial high-fidelity data generation.
