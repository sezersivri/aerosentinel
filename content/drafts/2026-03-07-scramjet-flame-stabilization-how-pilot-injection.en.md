This paper investigates the influence of pilot hot gas injection on flame stabilization within a cavity-based scramjet combustor. The authors employed three-dimensional unsteady Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes simulations to model ethylene fuel injection in a dual-solution regime, where two distinct flame stabilization modes could exist. The primary goal was to understand how pilot injection, at varying heating levels, affects both ignition and sustained flame stabilization, particularly focusing on potential mode transitions.

The simulations revealed that flame stabilization is highly sensitive to the pilot heating power. A low level of pilot heating maintained the flame in a cavity shear-layer stabilization mode. However, when the pilot heating was sufficiently high, it induced a transition to a jet-wake stabilization mode. This transition was primarily attributed to a reduction in the ignition delay within the unburned jet-wake region, caused by the elevated temperatures from the pilot. Additionally, pilot injection was found to enhance fuel–air mixing through the generation of vortices, with both the reduced ignition delay and mixing enhancement intensifying as the pilot heating power increased. Consequently, high heating power facilitated upstream flame propagation and flow separation, which ultimately triggered the observed mode transition. A theoretical analysis, based on Semenov thermal ignition theory, supported these findings by showing that high pilot heating promoted chemical heat release in the unburned jet-wake, exceeding the enthalpy outflow of the initial cavity shear-layer mode, leading to a sustained temperature rise and initiating the transition.

Understanding these flame stabilization mode transitions is important for the design and operation of scramjet engines. The ability to control or predict such transitions through pilot injection strategies directly impacts the reliability and performance envelope of high-speed propulsion systems, which are critical for applications like high-speed missiles.
