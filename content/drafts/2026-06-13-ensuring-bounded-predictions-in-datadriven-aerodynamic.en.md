This paper addresses a common challenge in data-driven low-order models for fluid dynamics: their tendency to produce unbounded predictions over extended time. The authors propose integrating theoretical advances concerning the long-term boundedness of dynamical systems into data-driven modeling frameworks. Specifically, they employ convex semidefinite programming to both certify if a system admits a globally attracting bounded set for given model parameters and to compute a model with the tightest possible bound on this set. This methodology is demonstrated by integrating it within the Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) framework.

The approach is initially validated on two low-order benchmark problems. The authors then apply their method to develop a six-mode low-order model for unsteady separation over a NACA-65(1)-412 aerofoil at a Reynolds number of 20,000. This flow regime has historically been difficult to model accurately using data-driven techniques. The resulting model is shown to accurately capture the dynamics of unsteady separation, with the crucial outcome that its predictions remain bounded indefinitely, unlike many traditional data-driven models that can diverge.

This work is significant for developing reliable, physically viable long-term forecasts from data-driven models of strongly nonlinear flows. Ensuring model boundedness is critical for applications in areas such as control system design or trajectory prediction, where model stability is a prerequisite for practical utility. While the application here is to incompressible, low Reynolds number flow, the underlying methodology for ensuring model stability could be extended to more complex high-speed aerodynamic phenomena.
