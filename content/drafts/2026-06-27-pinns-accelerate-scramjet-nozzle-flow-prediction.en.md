Tong and colleagues investigate a dual data- and knowledge-driven modeling approach, termed DE-PINN, for rapid prediction of scramjet nozzle flowfields. The work aims to overcome limitations in accuracy and physical interpretability often seen in purely data-driven models, particularly when dealing with skewed computational fluid dynamics (CFD) data distributions. They introduce a structured grid bilinear data enhancement (SGBDE) method to improve the input data characteristics. Concurrently, a physics-informed neural network (PINN) framework is developed, incorporating Euler equation constraints and flow physical quantity vector structure constraints to improve the model's generalization capabilities.

Experimental validation shows that DE-PINN achieves a coefficient of determination (R²) greater than 0.997 for predicting various physical quantities, which is a significant improvement over other models tested. Ablation studies confirm that both the SGBDE method and the physics-informed constraints contribute effectively to the model's performance, highlighting their synergistic interaction.

This method provides a valuable tool for accelerating the design and optimization of scramjet nozzles, where traditional CFD simulations are computationally intensive. The integration of physical laws directly into the neural network architecture represents a promising direction for developing more accurate and physically consistent predictive models for complex high-speed flow phenomena.
