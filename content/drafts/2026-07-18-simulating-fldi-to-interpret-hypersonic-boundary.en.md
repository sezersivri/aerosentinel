The paper assesses the capabilities of Focused Laser Differential Interferometry (FLDI) for measuring first- and second-mode boundary-layer instabilities in hypersonic flows. The authors conduct a computational analysis, using Direct Numerical Simulations (DNS) of Mach 6 flow over a cone to generate detailed density fields that contain both instability modes. They then simulate the FLDI measurement process by numerically integrating optical beam paths through these DNS data, producing synthetic time-series signals that mimic experimental outputs.

Analysis of these synthetic FLDI signals shows that the technique accurately captures the frequency content of the dominant instabilities. However, direct comparisons with the underlying DNS data reveal that FLDI's finite sampling volume acts as a spatial filter, which alters the measured amplitudes between the different instability modes. Specifically, the second mode was found to be amplified relative to the first mode, a phenomenon attributed to a combination of its wavelength and orientation within the flow.

This study confirms FLDI's utility for analyzing multimode instabilities and provides quantitative insights into the diagnostic's measurement characteristics. These findings are directly valuable for interpreting experimental campaigns aimed at understanding laminar-to-turbulent transition in hypersonic boundary layers, which is crucial for predicting aerothermal loads on high-speed vehicles.
