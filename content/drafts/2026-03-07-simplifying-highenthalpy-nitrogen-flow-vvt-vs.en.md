This paper investigates the impact of vibration–vibration–translation (VVT) reactions in state-to-state (StS) simulations of high-enthalpy nitrogen flow over a sphere. The primary objective is to reduce the significant computational cost associated with detailed StS simulations by evaluating two simplification methods: multiquantum restrictions and simplifying VVT transitions to vibration–translation (VTm) transitions. This work extends a previous study, systematically analyzing how these transition models affect flowfield characteristics and, crucially, heat flux distributions.

The numerical results demonstrate that both the full StS VVT model and the simplified VTm model predict nearly identical shock standoff distances and stagnation-point heat fluxes. These predictions align well with available experimental data. When comparing VVT and VTm transitions, the study finds minimal differences in the predictions of translational temperature, vibrational temperature, and nitrogen mass fraction. Near the wall, both models yield almost identical state populations, which exhibit gradually pronounced non-Boltzmann distributions.

The key finding is that VVT transitions can be effectively reduced to VTm transitions without compromising essential accuracy for predicting aerodynamic heating in this high-enthalpy nitrogen flow scenario. This outcome is valuable for the aerothermodynamics community, as it offers a practical approach to decrease the computational burden of detailed state-to-state simulations, making them more feasible for multidimensional applications while maintaining accuracy in critical heating predictions.
