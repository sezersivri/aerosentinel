This paper investigates how wall curvature influences the evolution of Mack modes in hypersonic boundary layers, a critical factor for boundary layer transition. The authors use linearized compressible Navier-Stokes equations to parametrically study flat-plate, concave, and convex curvatures, aiming to understand their impact on transition mechanisms. The work addresses a gap in understanding curvature effects on Mack modes in hypersonic flows, building upon existing research that primarily focuses on freestream parameters.

The study's findings indicate that convex curvature stabilizes Mack modes due to favorable pressure gradients, while concave curvature destabilizes them through adverse pressure gradients. As curvature increases, these effects become more pronounced: greater convex curvature further suppresses Mack modes, and greater concave curvature amplifies them. The research provides a detailed numerical analysis of these physical phenomena.

Understanding these curvature effects is important for the design of hypersonic configurations, as boundary layer transition significantly affects aerodynamic heating and vehicle performance. The insights gained offer guidance for managing transition location, which is crucial for thermal management and overall vehicle integrity.
