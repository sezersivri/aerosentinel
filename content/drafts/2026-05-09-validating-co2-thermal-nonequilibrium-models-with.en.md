This paper presents a time-resolved experimental and computational study of thermochemical nonequilibrium CO2 expanding flows, conducted in the JF-10 reflected shock tunnel. The authors employed tunable diode laser absorption spectroscopy (TDLAS) in both mid-infrared and near-infrared ranges to measure rotational and vibrational temperatures, as well as species concentrations. Water vapor was used as a tracer to identify driver-gas contamination, helping to define the effective test window for the experiments.

The study found a quasi-steady test period of approximately 0.6 ms, characterized by minimal contamination and stable thermochemical conditions. A two-temperature computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation, using the Eilmer4 solver, was performed to model the vibrational relaxation behavior of CO2. When comparing various relaxation models, the Simpson model provided the best agreement with the experimentally observed vibrational temperature evolution.

This work provides valuable benchmark data for CO2 test flows, particularly those relevant to planetary entry conditions. It also demonstrates the capability of TDLAS as a diagnostic tool for validating nonequilibrium flow models, which is crucial for improving the accuracy of aerothermodynamic predictions in high-speed flight regimes.
