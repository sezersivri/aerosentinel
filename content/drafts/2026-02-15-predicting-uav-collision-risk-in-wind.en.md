This paper introduces a methodology for predicting the probability of obstacle collision for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) operating in windy conditions. The approach centers on using Gaussian Process Regression to create a fast, approximate representation of wind fields along a predefined trajectory. This wind model then feeds into a 6-degrees-of-freedom UAV trajectory simulator, which incorporates a rotorcraft lumped-mass model and an LQRI controller to simulate trajectory deviations. The study considers both steady-state wind and dynamic wind gust effects.

The authors demonstrate the method using real flight data from an octocopter at NASA Langley Research Center. They compute collision probabilities against simulated obstacles under various wind conditions. The work also explores the impact of varying wind conditions and UAV airspeeds, correlating these with experimental flights and data from ground-based weather stations.

The presented approach offers a way to quickly assess safety thresholds, such as minimum separation from obstacles or available battery resources, by accounting for wind-induced trajectory deviations. This capability supports risk-informed decision-making for managing safety-critical events in autonomous systems.
