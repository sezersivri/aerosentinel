This study investigates the viscous shock tube, a standard test case for Navier-Stokes (NS) solvers, focusing on low Reynolds number conditions. The authors demonstrate that significant non-equilibrium phenomena can occur even in nominally continuum flow regimes, challenging the traditional assumption of near-equilibrium behavior inherent in NS-based modeling. To address this, they employ the unified gas-kinetic scheme (UGKS), a multiscale method, and compare its results against solutions obtained from the conventional gas-kinetic scheme (GKS), which is NS-based.

The comparison between UGKS and GKS solutions reveals clear discrepancies, particularly in regions where shock waves interact with boundary layers. These differences are attributed to pronounced non-equilibrium effects that the NS-based GKS fails to fully capture. The abstract does not provide specific numerical values for Mach numbers, Reynolds numbers, or quantitative error metrics.

The work highlights the importance of using multiscale methods like UGKS for accurate analysis and prediction of continuum flows at high Mach and low Reynolds numbers, where such non-equilibrium transport becomes critical. This has direct implications for the fidelity of CFD simulations in aerothermodynamics, especially for high-speed missile applications where accurate prediction of flow physics is essential for heating calculations.
