Jung and Towne extend a resolvent-based framework for estimating turbulent velocity fluctuations, applying it to the turbulent wake of a NACA0012 airfoil at Mach 0.3, Reynolds number 23,000, and 6 degrees angle of attack. The approach addresses challenges of globally unstable modes, multi-scale turbulent structures, and high-dimensional datasets. They construct causal resolvent-based estimation kernels from cross-spectral densities derived from large-eddy simulations, employing the Wiener–Hopf method to enforce causality.

The framework effectively captures the spectral signatures of coherent structures and implicitly accounts for the colored statistics of nonlinear forcing through empirically determined cross-spectral densities. To manage computational demands for high-dimensional estimation, parallel algorithms are employed. The authors also investigate sensor placement by analyzing single-sensor estimation error and coherence with target flow quantities. Results indicate accurate causal estimation of streamwise velocity for the spanwise-averaged, spanwise-Fourier-transformed, and mid-span flow, based on limited shear-stress measurements on the airfoil surface.

This work demonstrates the potential of the resolvent-based framework for efficient flow estimation in compressible, turbulent environments. While the specific application is a low-speed turbulent wake, the methodology for data-driven flow estimation and understanding complex turbulent structures could inform broader efforts in analyzing and controlling high-speed flows.
