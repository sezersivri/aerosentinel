**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 65/100

This paper presents an experimental investigation into the hypersonic flow over 30°–50° double-cone configurations, examining the impact of three distinct nose bluntness levels (sharp, small-blunt, large-blunt) at Mach 6. The researchers employed high-speed schlieren photography, pressure sensors, and pressure-sensitive paint (PSP) to characterize both global flow patterns and unsteady dynamics within a transitional Reynolds number regime. The study's primary goal was to understand how nose bluntness influences the complex shock-boundary layer interactions and flow unsteadiness inherent to these geometries.

The experimental findings indicate that increasing nose bluntness leads to an enlargement of the separation region at the cone junction. All three configurations exhibited Type V shock–shock interactions, but the shock wave structures downstream of the triple point varied: Mach shock wave reflection was observed in the sharp and small-blunt cases, while regular shock wave reflection occurred in the large-blunt case. Spectral analysis of the schlieren sequences revealed two types of unsteadiness: low-frequency shock oscillations and high-frequency structures along the supersonic jet boundary. For the low-frequency unsteadiness, sharp and small-blunt configurations showed a broadband nature, whereas the large-blunt case displayed a dominant frequency of approximately 2 kHz, associated with shock motion and bubble breathing. Wall pressure contours confirmed that this low-frequency unsteadiness was predominantly axisymmetric across all configurations. Global stability and resolvent analyses further suggested that all configurations act as noise amplifiers, with the dominant low-frequency unsteadiness in the large-blunt case attributed to modal resonance.

Understanding the influence of nose bluntness on shock-boundary layer interaction and associated unsteadiness is important for the design and analysis of high-speed missiles. The observed unsteady phenomena directly contribute to fluctuating pressure and thermal loads, which are critical considerations for structural integrity and thermal protection system performance.
