This paper examines the broadband turbulent dynamics of attached and separated flows over a Gaussian bump, specifically focusing on the origins of low-frequency coherent structures. The authors combine time-resolved experimental measurements with physics-based linear models, employing mean fields assimilated from the experimental data as base flows for their analysis. This approach aims to provide a deeper understanding of complex turbulent phenomena in these flow configurations.

Through spectral proper orthogonal decomposition, the study identifies coherent dynamics in both low- and medium-frequency regimes for both attached and separated flows, with the low-frequency dynamics being considerably more pronounced in the separated case. For the separated flow, these dynamics are attributed to a three-dimensional zero-frequency modal instability that generates large-scale, streamwise-elongated structures downstream of the bump. A standing-wave model, derived from resolvent modes and accounting for finite-span effects, successfully reproduces the experimentally observed spanwise structure of these dynamics. The paper does not provide specific quantitative metrics such as Mach numbers or error rates, as its focus is on fundamental flow physics.

The findings highlight the limitations of simulations with small spanwise extents and periodic boundary conditions, particularly for separated flows. By identifying the three-dimensional zero-frequency instability and finite-span standing-wave dynamics as key drivers of low-frequency coherent structures, the work offers an explanation for persistent discrepancies between simulations and experiments on the Gaussian bump. This provides valuable guidance for setting appropriate spanwise domain sizes and boundary conditions in future computational fluid dynamics studies of similar flows.
