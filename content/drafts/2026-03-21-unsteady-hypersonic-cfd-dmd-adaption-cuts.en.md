This paper addresses the computational expense and accuracy challenges of simulating unsteady supersonic and hypersonic flows, particularly concerning mesh refinement. The authors propose a method to improve anisotropic grid adaptation for unsteady flowfields by integrating Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) into the adaptation process. DMD, which is linked to the Koopman operator, is used as a data-driven technique to model complex nonlinear flows and dynamically adjust the mesh in response to evolving flow features, aiming to maintain accuracy with less computational complexity.

The effectiveness of this approach was demonstrated through numerical experiments on configurations such as a cylinder in subsonic flow and an oscillating cylinder in a supersonic channel flow. The results indicate that incorporating DMD enables an accurate representation of unsteady flow dynamics, independent of the remeshing interval. The dynamic anisotropic mesh adaptation achieved a fourfold reduction in drag error when compared to traditional static meshing methods.

This method offers a way to improve the efficiency and accuracy of unsteady CFD simulations for high-speed applications. By reducing computational cost and improving accuracy in capturing dynamic features, it could indirectly benefit downstream analyses requiring precise unsteady flowfields, such as those informing aerothermodynamic predictions.
