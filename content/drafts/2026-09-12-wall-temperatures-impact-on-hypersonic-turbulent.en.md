Fratini and Bernardini investigate the influence of wall temperature on a turbulent hypersonic boundary layer operating under chemical non-equilibrium conditions. They perform Direct Numerical Simulations (DNS) of a spatially evolving, zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layer. The simulated flow corresponds to Mach 10 with a high stagnation enthalpy, sufficient to activate thermochemical processes. The study considers three isothermal, non-catalytic wall conditions, with wall temperatures ranging from 1400 K to 2000 K.

The simulations demonstrate that wall cooling promotes an earlier transition to turbulence and leads to a monotonic increase in wall heat flux. Mean-velocity profiles collapse satisfactorily under modern compressibility transformations. The thermal field exhibits two distinct modifications with stronger wall cooling: a reduction of the mean temperature profile and an outward shift of the wall-normal position of the temperature peak. These thermal modifications significantly impact both turbulence statistics and chemical activity. By lowering the mean temperature, wall cooling suppresses endothermic chemical reactions and reduces the associated energy drain, allowing larger temperature fluctuations to develop. This results in a reduction of dissociation product concentration by up to a factor of two between the hottest and coldest wall conditions.

This work clarifies the critical role of wall temperature in shaping the thermal field and regulating chemical activity within hypersonic turbulent boundary layers. Understanding these coupled effects is important for accurate aerodynamic heating predictions and for designing effective thermal management strategies for high-speed vehicles.
