This paper investigates passive control for drag reduction on a simplified road vehicle geometry, specifically the square-back Windsor body, through the optimization of a rear roof extension. The study employs high-fidelity wall-resolved large eddy simulation (LES) to model the flow. The optimization process relies on Gaussian process-based surrogate modeling combined with Bayesian optimization, guided by an expected improvement criterion, to efficiently explore the design space defined by the roof extension's taper penetration distance, angle of incidence, and length.

The optimization converged within six iterations, requiring a total of 60 LES simulations, and resulted in a 6.5% reduction in drag. The authors identified six distinct mechanisms contributing to this drag reduction, including diffuser-induced pressure recovery, base-size reduction, and modifications to the vertical wake balance. The optimal configuration was found when the roof extension angle corresponded to the onset of separation, with both the taper penetration distance and extension length at their maximum values within the analyzed domain.

This work provides a framework for aerodynamic optimization, demonstrating how coupled wake–geometry interactions can be effectively managed for improved aerodynamic performance. While the application is to road vehicles, the methodology of using Gaussian processes for design optimization with high-fidelity CFD is relevant to broader engineering design challenges.
