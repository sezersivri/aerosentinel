**Type:** 💻 Numerical/CFD | **Relevance:** 60/100

This paper investigates the use of passive control bumps to mitigate low-frequency unsteadiness within shock-boundary layer interactions. Direct numerical simulations are conducted for a Mach 2 turbulent boundary layer subjected to a 12-degree oblique shock. The study specifically assesses how a shock control bump, designed based on the baseline interaction, influences the dynamics of the separation bubble and wall-pressure fluctuations.

Results show that the uncontrolled interaction exhibits a significant breathing motion of the separation bubble, characterized by dominant low-frequency wall-pressure energy. The introduction of the nominal shock control bump effectively confines separation to smaller, localized regions and reduces overall wall-pressure fluctuations across the interaction. It also suppresses the low-frequency component at the reflected-shock foot, shifting spectral energy towards higher frequencies. While a secondary unsteadiness emerges near the bump apex at these higher frequencies, it does not lead to a return of large-scale oscillations. Sensitivity studies on bump placement indicate that an upstream shift largely preserves the low-frequency mitigation, whereas a downstream shift worsens separation and reintroduces strong low-frequency peaks.

This work provides insight into a physical mechanism where the bump's geometry constrains the reflected shock, creating a localized compression and expansion that reduces shock mobility. Understanding and controlling SBLI unsteadiness is important for high-speed missile design, as these phenomena can impact structural integrity and control effectiveness, even if the primary focus here is not on thermal loads.
