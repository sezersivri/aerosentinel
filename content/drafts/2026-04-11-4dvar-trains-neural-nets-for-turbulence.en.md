**Type:** 🤖 ML/Aerodynamics | **Relevance:** 82/100

This paper introduces a method for state estimation in three-dimensional homogeneous isotropic turbulence by training neural networks for super-resolution using a 4DVar data assimilation algorithm. The key innovation is that this approach eliminates the need for high-resolution 'ground truth' data, which is typically required for machine learning-based super-resolution. Instead, the neural network weights are optimized by minimizing a loss function that incorporates entire trajectories of coarse observations, enabled by a fully differentiable pseudo-spectral JAX-CFD solver combined with a convolutional neural network.

The study demonstrates that the trained neural networks generally outperform a standalone 4DVar algorithm for state estimation at the initial time, although 4DVar provides more robust predictions towards the end of its assimilation window. A hybrid approach, where the neural network output initializes 4DVar, yields significantly improved performance, achieving more than twice the accuracy of other state estimation strategies across all times. This hybrid model also performs well even beyond known limiting length scales, all without requiring any high-resolution measurements during training or operation.

This work offers a valuable strategy for flow reconstruction in data-scarce environments, which is highly relevant for understanding complex turbulent phenomena. The ability to train high-fidelity flow models without extensive high-resolution data could accelerate the development of predictive tools for turbulent boundary layers and their associated heating effects, particularly in scenarios where high-fidelity CFD or experimental data is prohibitively expensive or unavailable.
