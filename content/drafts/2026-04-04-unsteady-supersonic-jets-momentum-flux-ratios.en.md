**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 35/100

This experimental study investigates the behavior of elliptical liquid jets injected into a supersonic cross-flow at Mach 2.5. The authors examine how the momentum flux ratio (J) and orifice aspect ratio (AR) affect the jet breakup mechanism, shock structures, and overall unsteadiness. Three aspect ratios (0.3, 1, and 3.3) and a wide range of momentum flux ratios (1.5–9.7) are considered.

The findings indicate that lower momentum flux ratios lead to significant jet unsteadiness, characterized by larger wavelength Rayleigh–Taylor (RT) waves on the windward surface. Conversely, increasing J reduces unsteadiness and promotes the formation of smaller, more regular RT waves due to enhanced drag from reduced jet deflection. For aspect ratios of 0.3 and 1, Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities on the lateral surfaces are identified as the primary atomization mechanism, irrespective of J. Furthermore, at lower J values, the shock waves upstream of the jet exhibit considerable corrugation and temporal variation, with intense interaction between the liquid jet and oncoming boundary layer streaks driving the large-scale unsteadiness.

This work provides insights into the complex fluid dynamics of liquid jets in supersonic environments, which is relevant for understanding phenomena such as fuel injection in high-speed propulsion systems or liquid-based flow control. While not directly focused on aerodynamic heating, it contributes to the broader understanding of high-speed flow interactions.
