**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 42/100

This study experimentally investigates the unsteady interaction between a normal shock wave and a turbulent boundary layer at an upstream Mach number of 1.35. The research was conducted in the S8Ch transonic wind tunnel at ONERA Meudon, employing wall-mounted unsteady pressure measurements and high-speed schlieren imaging to characterize the shock-boundary layer interaction (SBLI).

The experiments identified a small separation region along the centerline. Spectral analysis of the reconstructed wall pressure signals revealed distinct frequency ranges associated with different flow features: the shock wave exhibited unsteadiness around a Strouhal number of 0.01, peaking at approximately 0.013; the separation point showed activity between St ≈ 0.04 and 0.25; and the mixing layer displayed frequencies with St ≳ 0.3. It was observed that in the separation region, time scales linked to both shock motion and the mixing layer coexist. Spectral Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (SPOD) of schlieren images further elucidated coherent structures within the shear layer and low-frequency unsteadiness related to the shock motion. The authors also highlight, through synthetic image analysis, that sharp discontinuities like shocks can introduce additional modes at higher harmonics, emphasizing the need for careful interpretation in SBLI studies.

Understanding the unsteady nature of shock-boundary layer interactions, even at transonic speeds, is foundational for predicting flow behavior in more complex supersonic and hypersonic environments. While not directly focused on heating, the detailed characterization of flow structures and unsteadiness provides critical data for validating CFD models that are ultimately used to predict aerodynamic heating and structural loads on high-speed vehicles.
