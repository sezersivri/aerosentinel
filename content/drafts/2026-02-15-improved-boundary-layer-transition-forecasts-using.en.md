This paper explores methods to enhance Artificial Neural Network (ANN) models for predicting boundary layer transition, specifically focusing on Tollmien-Schlichting (TS) instability waves in subsonic boundary layers. The authors aimed to improve the accuracy of ANNs in forecasting the amplification rates of these waves, which are fundamental to determining transition location. Their approach involved applying recent machine learning developments, including surrogate optimization techniques and strategic augmentation of the training data, to refine the ANN models.

The optimized ANN models, trained on the modified dataset, demonstrated a significant improvement in prediction accuracy. They reduced the average transition location errors on various airfoils and flow conditions by 51% compared to the original manually-tuned network. These transition locations were benchmarked against results from the Langley Stability and Transition Analysis Code (LASTRAC).

This work contributes to making linear stability theory-based transition prediction more computationally efficient and less user-intensive for integration into computational fluid dynamics codes, particularly for general aviation applications where TS waves are the dominant transition mechanism.
