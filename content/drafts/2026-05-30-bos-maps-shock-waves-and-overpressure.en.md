**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 55/100

This paper details the application of background-oriented schlieren (BOS) for quantitatively characterizing shock waves generated by a free-field outdoor gas-detonation-powered pressure relief system. The authors developed a BOS setup, addressing challenges like natural background interference by using an optimized random dot pattern. They also accounted for the intense radiation emanating from the high-enthalpy gas flow, which is a critical consideration for accurate optical measurements in such environments.

The primary objective was to visualize the shock wave front and reconstruct the corresponding overpressure field. A linear calibration was established between the optical BOS results and data from subsidiary pressure sensors. The estimated peak overpressures derived from the BOS measurements showed good agreement with the directly measured values, with BOS estimates slightly underpredicting by a maximum observed deviation of 8%. This demonstrates the method's capability to determine peak overpressure in locations where physical sensors cannot be placed due to experimental constraints.

This work provides a valuable experimental technique for characterizing complex shock wave phenomena in high-enthalpy flows, which is relevant for understanding the flow fields around high-speed vehicles. The ability to reconstruct pressure fields optically extends the diagnostic capabilities for studying transient, high-energy events, offering insights into blast wave propagation and interaction with structures, which is indirectly applicable to missile aerothermodynamics and design considerations.
