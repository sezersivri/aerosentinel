This paper presents a comparative analysis of several boundary-layer stability codes applied to hypersonic, high-enthalpy flows over blunted cones. The study focuses on two geometries: a 7-degree and a 10.9-degree half-angle blunted cone. The primary objective is to assess the influence of different numerical approaches on second-mode instability and to characterize the role of high-temperature gas effects. The computational results from seven distinct stability codes are compared against experimental data obtained from the High Enthalpy Shock Tunnel (HIEST) in Japan and the High-Enthalpy Laboratory Munich (HELM) in Germany.

The analysis quantifies the impact of high-temperature gas effects on the second mode and investigates the influence of uncertainties, such as minor variations in mean-flow conditions or wall temperature. A specific comparison is also performed between air and CO2 test gases, highlighting differences in stability characteristics under varying gas compositions. The paper provides insights into the agreement and discrepancies among the various computational methods and their ability to reproduce experimental observations under high-enthalpy conditions.

This work is significant for the aerothermodynamics community as it enhances confidence in computational tools used for predicting boundary-layer stability and transition on high-speed vehicles. A better understanding of second-mode instability and real gas effects in high-enthalpy flows is crucial for accurate aerodynamic heating predictions and the design of effective thermal protection systems for hypersonic missiles and reentry vehicles.
