**Type:** 💻 Numerical/CFD | **Relevance:** 65/100

This paper investigates the effectiveness of spanwise heterogeneous, ridge-type wall roughness in passively controlling shock-wave/turbulent boundary-layer interactions (STBLIs). The authors employed wall-resolved large-eddy simulations (LES) to analyze a Mach 2.0 impinging STBLI flow with a 40-degree impinging-shock angle. They considered incoming turbulent boundary layers at two friction Reynolds numbers, approximately 350 and 1200, and explored multiple roughness configurations scaled by either inner or outer flow parameters.

The results indicate that while the rough-wall configurations introduce a moderate increase in mean drag, they substantially alter the dynamics of the interaction. The wall-pressure fluctuations near the separation-shock foot, which consist of low-frequency components from large-scale shock excursions and high-frequency components from amplified turbulence, were significantly attenuated. At the lower Reynolds number, both low- and high-frequency components contributed comparably to the overall reduction. At the higher Reynolds number, a stronger overall reduction of the pressure fluctuation peak was observed, primarily due to the effective suppression of the low-frequency component. Cross-correlation analyses suggest downstream mechanisms drive the low-frequency dynamics in this strong interaction regime, where large-scale shock excursions are mainly driven by the breathing of the reverse-flow bubble. Large-scale Görtler-like vortices were identified around the reattachment location in all cases, appearing largely unaffected by roughness geometry.

This work provides valuable insights into passive flow control strategies for STBLIs, which are critical phenomena in high-speed flight. Understanding how wall roughness can modify turbulence and shock dynamics offers potential avenues for mitigating adverse effects in supersonic and hypersonic vehicle designs, particularly in areas prone to strong shock interactions.
