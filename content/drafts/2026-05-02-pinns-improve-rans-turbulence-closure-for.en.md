This paper addresses the long-standing challenge of inaccurate turbulence closure in traditional Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) models, particularly for flows where the Boussinesq eddy-viscosity hypothesis falls short. The authors focus on flow past a circular cylinder across Reynolds numbers from 3900 to 100,000 and Mach numbers from 0 to 0.3, covering incompressible and weakly compressible regimes. Their approach involves developing a data-driven closure by assembling a cross-validated dataset from hydrodynamic PIV, aerodynamic PIV, and high-fidelity DNS and LES data.

The core methodology employs physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) to infer the velocity field and Reynolds-stress forcing directly from boundary conditions, with the unclosed RANS equations embedded in the network's loss function. Analysis of the assembled data revealed a universal distribution of Reynolds stresses across the studied parameter space, which informed the data-driven closure. The resulting PINN-derived closure, when integrated into a forward PINN solver and the OpenFOAM numerical solver, significantly improves RANS predictions for both mean flow and turbulence statistics compared to conventional models.

While the study's focus is on low-speed, incompressible flows around a cylinder, the methodology for improving RANS turbulence closure using data-driven methods and PINNs is relevant to the broader CFD community. Accurate turbulence modeling is a foundational requirement for predicting complex flow phenomena, including those encountered in high-speed aerothermodynamics, even if this specific paper does not directly address heating or hypersonic regimes.
