This paper presents a low-fidelity optimization framework designed to assist in the initial design phase of high-speed reentry capsules and vehicles. The framework integrates several models, including those for reentry trajectory simulation, hypersonic shock structure formation, spatiotemporal aerothermal load prediction, and conjugate heat transfer to the vehicle. These interconnected effects are incorporated into the open-source Stanford University Aerospace Vehicle Environment (SUAVE) framework, which facilitates vehicle design and trajectory optimization.

The work first evaluates the accuracy of its various aerothermal modeling assumptions for both sharp and blunt geometries. Following this validation, the framework is applied to a realistic reentry case, where it is used to optimize the vehicle's geometry and trajectory with the objective of minimizing the peak surface temperature experienced during reentry.

This approach provides a rapid method for initial design considerations, particularly where quick assessments of aerothermal loads and their influence on thermal protection systems are essential. By combining various low-fidelity models, the framework enables efficient exploration of design spaces and mission profiles, offering a valuable tool before more computationally intensive high-fidelity simulations are undertaken.
