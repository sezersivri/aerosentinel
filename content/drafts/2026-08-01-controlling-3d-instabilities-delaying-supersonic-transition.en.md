This paper investigates a robust control law for three-dimensional instabilities in a supersonic boundary layer at Mach 4.5, aiming to delay transition to turbulence. The methodology extends previous work on spanwise invariant flow to the 3D case, employing localized sensors and actuators. The control law is synthesized using linear tools, transforming a multiple-input–multiple-output problem into several decentralized single-input–single-output problems in the transverse wavenumber space to simplify identification and synthesis.

The study demonstrates that a feedback setup, combined with structured H2/Hinf synthesis, ensures nominal performance and robustness against noisy estimation sensors or freestream velocity variations. By simultaneously targeting the linear growth of both the first and second instability modes, the control law successfully delays transition to turbulence. Specifically, it increases the laminar zone by 20% from the upstream edge of the plate.

This work is significant for the aerothermodynamics community as delaying transition to turbulence on high-speed vehicles directly impacts aerodynamic heating. Maintaining laminar flow for a longer duration can substantially reduce skin friction drag and, critically, the overall heat flux experienced by the vehicle, which is a major design consideration for high-speed missiles.
