**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 15/100

This paper systematically assesses the robustness and accuracy of multi-exposure (ME) 3D particle tracking velocimetry (3D-PTV), a technique designed to overcome limitations of single-exposure double-frame recordings and reduce system requirements compared to time-resolved (TR) systems. The authors scrutinize particle tracking reliability and accuracy in ME recordings with up to five exposures, distributed over one or two frames, using a synthetic particle field based on a Taylor–Green vortex lattice. The study also simulates ME recordings from a time-resolved experiment around a wall-mounted cube and demonstrates the method for accelerometry and pressure from PIV in a turbulent wake.

The findings indicate that single-frame ME yields viable results, achieving a 90% detection rate up to a cumulated particle image density of 0.1. However, the error rate remains below 5% only when exposures are distributed over two frames. The dynamic velocity range (DVR) of ME systems is found to be approximately equivalent to that of time-resolved systems. The dynamic acceleration range (DAR), on the other hand, only approaches TR analysis capabilities for specific combinations of pulse numbers and timing sequences. The simulated ME (4 pulses, 2 frames) shows equivalence to TR conditions for the wall-mounted cube case.

This work provides a detailed characterization of an advanced experimental fluid measurement technique. While not directly focused on high-speed missile aerothermodynamics or surrogate modeling for heating prediction, the insights into improving flow field measurement accuracy could indirectly support validation efforts for computational fluid dynamics in various aerospace applications.
