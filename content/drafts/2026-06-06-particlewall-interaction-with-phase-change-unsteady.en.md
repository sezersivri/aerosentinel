This paper models the unsteady planar motion of a rigid particle flying near a wall-embedded substrate that is undergoing solidification or melting. The authors simplify the fluid flow equations to a thin-layer form, assuming both the particle and the particle–substrate gap are thin. Additionally, they exploit the small Prandtl number limit to derive a closed-form solution for the fluid temperature, significantly reducing the complexity of the coupled system.

The numerical simulations of this interactive system reveal four distinct terminal behaviors for the particle: a near-collision with the substrate boundary at the front, middle, or back of the particle, or a fly-away scenario where the particle departs from the substrate. For the specific case of a mid-body collision, a detailed local analysis uncovers an intricate asymptotic structure within the model solutions. The study also extends its examination to the inverse scenario, where the substrate is rigid, and the particle itself is melting or solidifying.

While not directly focused on external aerodynamic heating of high-speed missiles, this work provides fundamental insights into particle-surface interactions coupled with phase change. Such mechanisms could be relevant to understanding material response, like ablation or erosion, in extreme thermal environments, offering a simplified framework for complex surface-fluid phenomena.
