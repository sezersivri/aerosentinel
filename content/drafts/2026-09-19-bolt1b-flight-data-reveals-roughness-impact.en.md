This paper details the BOLT-1B flight experiment, part of an ongoing series to investigate hypersonic boundary layer transition and turbulence. The experiment used a geometry featuring concave surfaces and highly swept leading edges, designed to allow simultaneous smooth and rough wall investigations. The authors specifically focus on the rough wall flight data from BOLT-1B, examining the impact of nose-joint steps on transition onset. They provide details on the as-flown size and locations of these steps.

The key finding is that the rough side consistently exhibited a lower transition Reynolds number compared to the smooth side, aligning with expected behavior. The flight data for rough wall transition also showed good agreement with the ground-based step correlation that was used in the experiment's design. Specific quantitative metrics like Mach numbers, precise Reynolds numbers, or error percentages are not provided in the abstract.

This work provides valuable flight validation for understanding the effects of surface roughness on hypersonic boundary layer transition. Such data are crucial for refining predictive models and design methodologies for high-speed vehicles, where accurate transition prediction is essential for thermal management and aerodynamic performance.
