This paper investigates elastic travelling-wave solutions, referred to as 'arrowheads,' within a viscoelastic, unidirectionally body-forced flow. The authors focus on the existence and morphological evolution of these solutions as the Weissenberg number (Wi) and streamwise duct length (L) are varied. The study explores the branch topology and the characteristics of these complex flow structures.

The findings indicate that the branch topology transitions from an isola at low L, through a two-sided reconnection at intermediate L, to a branch that persists at asymptotically large Wi for larger L. At intermediate L, more than two arrowhead solutions can coexist due to additional saddle–node bifurcations. The canonical arrowhead structure consists of two legs joined by an arched head that obstructs throughflow and traps a counter-rotating vortex pair. Polymer strands can also emerge from strong extensional regions associated with the arrowhead arch. The minimal domain length, L_min, required to sustain an arrowhead varies non-monotonically with Wi; for Wi ≥ 20, detached-strand states govern L_min, following the relation L_min ≈ 0.125 Wi + 1.5. In sufficiently long domains, the upper branch develops into a localized single arrowhead whose streamwise extent depends on Wi, while the lower branch can proliferate into a train of arrowheads at high Wi, a phenomenon not previously documented.
