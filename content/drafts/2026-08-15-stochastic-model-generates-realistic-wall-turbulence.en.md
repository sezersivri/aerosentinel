This paper extends a stochastic model for generating two-dimensional turbulent velocity fields within rough-wall boundary layers. Building on previous work that modeled uniform momentum zones (UMZs) and internal shear layers, the current study introduces small-scale swirling motions. These motions are generated stochastically using vortex cores, which are strategically placed and stretched with respect to the internal shear layers to capture the smallest flow scales.

The enhanced synthetic fields successfully reproduce second-order turbulence statistics and the energy spectrum across most of the inertial range. For wind tunnel datasets, the model captures the transition to exponential decay up to k1η ~ 0.2. This demonstrates the model's capability to represent both large-scale coherent structures and fine-scale turbulent eddies with reasonable accuracy.

The long-term objective of this research is to improve wall modeling strategies for Large Eddy Simulations (LES). By providing a statistically accurate, bottom-up reconstruction of rough-wall turbulence, this approach could lead to more accurate and efficient LES, particularly in applications where wall roughness significantly influences the flow physics. While not directly focused on high-speed aerothermodynamics, improved turbulence modeling is foundational for accurate heat flux predictions in CFD.
