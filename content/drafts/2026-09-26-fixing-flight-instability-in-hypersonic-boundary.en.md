**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 65/100

This paper examines the flight instability encountered during the Boundary Layer Transition Experiment (BOLT)-1A, an asymmetric hypersonic payload designed to measure boundary-layer transition. The vehicle experienced a significant roll-pitch lock-in condition shortly after first-stage separation, which compromised the experiment's performance. The authors identify an angle-of-attack divergence, triggered when the asymmetric vehicle crosses resonance, as the precursor to this observed roll-pitch lock-in.

The work introduces metrics to quantify the severity of unstable resonance crossing and distinguishes between small-amplitude and large-amplitude lock-in, with the latter providing the mechanism for the sustained instability seen in BOLT-1A. In contrast, the subsequent BOLT-1B flight successfully avoided this roll-pitch lock-in. The paper details the specific strategy employed on BOLT-1B to ensure a stable resonance crossing, which proved effective in preventing the large-amplitude instability.

The findings are significant for the design and execution of future asymmetric hypersonic flight experiments, particularly those focused on sensitive measurements like boundary layer transition. Understanding and mitigating such flight instabilities is crucial for obtaining reliable aerothermodynamic data in complex flow regimes.
