**Type:** 📐 Analytical | **Relevance:** 40/100

This paper models the unsteady response of nozzles subject to acoustic and entropy waves, considering the effects of wall friction. It uses the quasi-one-dimensional linearized Euler equations, reformulated in terms of dimensionless mass, stagnation temperature, and entropy fluctuations. The authors solve this first-order system of differential equations using the Magnus-expansion method, where perturbation parameters include normalized frequencies, Fanning friction factors, and, for the first time, a measure of flow non-isentropicity caused by wall friction.

The analytical solution is applied to a converging-diverging nozzle for both subsonic and supersonic flow conditions, demonstrating good agreement with numerical predictions. The study further investigates nozzle flow with combined wall friction and steady heat transfer. Key findings indicate that the acoustic and entropy transfer functions of the nozzle are strongly dependent on frequency, wall friction, and the rate of heat transfer. Wall friction influences the unsteady response through modifications to the mean flow and via coupling mechanisms between acoustic, velocity, and entropy fluctuations.

This work provides a detailed analytical framework for understanding complex unsteady phenomena in internal flows, particularly how friction and heat transfer modulate acoustic and entropy wave propagation in nozzles. While not directly addressing external aerodynamic heating on missiles, the insights into coupled thermal and fluid dynamics in high-speed internal flows could inform broader aerothermodynamic modeling efforts, especially in propulsion system design.
