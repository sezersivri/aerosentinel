This paper investigates the limitations of conventional steady Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) simulations when predicting the aerodynamic performance of multistage axial compressors, particularly those with large clearances. The study focuses on a 4-stage high-speed Mitsubishi Heavy Industries compressor, representing the middle-rear stages of a gas turbine compressor. The authors found that standard RANS models tend to over-predict 3D tip blockage and associated losses in the front stages of large-clearance configurations, which leads to an inaccurate, premature prediction of stall onset.

To address these issues, the paper proposes an advanced turbulence model combination. This includes a quadratic constitutive relation (QCR) correction, which relaxes the linear constitutive relation assumption of the Boussinesq approximation, and a vortical pressure gradient correction that accounts for vortex stretching effects in non-equilibrium 3D separated flows. This refined model significantly reduces prediction errors compared to conventional RANS. Specifically, it improves the total pressure ratio prediction error by approximately 76%, isentropic efficiency by 50%, and stall margin by 77%. The model also demonstrates strong predictive accuracy for overall performance and stage matching across both small and large clearance compressors.

This work offers a practical method for enhancing the reliability of RANS simulations in compressor design cycles. By providing more accurate performance and stall predictions, the proposed turbulence model can support the development of future small-core engine compressors, reducing the reliance on more computationally expensive, higher-fidelity methods for initial design iterations.
