**Type:** 🧪 Experimental | **Relevance:** 65/100

This paper describes the meticulous design and measurement efforts undertaken for the joint steps on the BOLT-1B flight experiment. The primary objective of BOLT-1B is to investigate hypersonic boundary-layer transition on geometries featuring low-curvature concave surfaces and highly swept leading edges. Recognizing the significant sensitivity of hypersonic boundary layers to surface roughness, a substantial focus was placed on controlling and documenting the roughness characteristics, particularly for two critical joint steps located on the upstream portion of the BOLT geometry. The authors developed novel methods specifically for measuring the small magnitude step heights typical of sounding rocket flight experiments.

Measurements of these step heights were systematically performed during the fabrication process, environmental testing, and preflight checkout. The initial findings confirm that all measured step heights were rearward-facing and remained within the specified design limits, which are crucial for accommodating differential thermal expansion during flight. The study successfully quantified both the magnitude and azimuthal distribution of these step heights across multiple joints of the flight experiment.

This work is important for the aerothermodynamics community as it addresses a fundamental challenge in experimental hypersonic research: ensuring the quality and fidelity of surface geometry for boundary layer transition studies. Accurate characterization of surface imperfections like joint steps is essential for generating reliable flight data that can be used to validate computational fluid dynamics models and enhance our understanding of transition mechanisms in high-speed flows.
