René Petervari and colleagues conducted an experimental parameter study investigating plasma formation and its impact on radar signatures around generic sphere-cone geometries. The experiments were performed under varying high enthalpy flow conditions in the High Enthalpy Shock Tunnel (HEG) at DLR Göttingen. This work was guided by combined Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) and Computational Electromagnetic (CEM) simulations, which first predicted plasma distribution and then determined the microwave response.

The primary goal was to generate quantitative reference data for plasma state and radar signature predictions, which are challenging to simulate with high precision. The measurements obtained from the series of sphere-cone geometries were then used to experimentally assess the coupled CFD-CEM framework's ability to predict radar signatures for plasma-shrouded hypersonic vehicles. The abstract does not provide specific numerical results or performance metrics for the assessment.

This study provides valuable experimental data for validating and improving computational models of plasma formation and its electromagnetic effects in hypersonic flight. Such data are crucial for enhancing the accuracy of radar signature predictions and understanding communication blackout phenomena for high-speed vehicles.
