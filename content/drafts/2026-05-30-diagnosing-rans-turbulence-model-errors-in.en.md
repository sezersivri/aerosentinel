**Type:** 💻 Numerical/CFD | **Relevance:** 38/100

This paper introduces a diagnostics framework designed to systematically isolate and quantify errors in turbulence models, specifically focusing on skin friction in wall-bounded turbulent boundary layers. The authors propose an angular momentum integral formulation to break down the skin friction coefficient into contributions from viscous effects, turbulence, pressure gradients, and mean-flow development. This method is applied to five transport-type Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) models across two test cases: a zero-pressure-gradient flat-plate boundary layer and flow over a three-dimensional hill.

For the flat-plate case, comparing against direct numerical simulations (DNS) data reveals that while all RANS models reproduce the skin friction coefficient reasonably well, this often occurs due to strong error cancellation, particularly between turbulent torque and mean-flux contributions. Individual terms can deviate by more than 20% of the skin friction coefficient. In the more complex hill case, where wall-resolved large-eddy simulations (LES) serve as the reference, errors are significantly larger. The dominant erroneous contribution varies by model and can exceed several times the local skin friction coefficient, depending on the streamwise position. In separated-flow regions, the error cancellation observed in the flat-plate case largely disappears, and the primary source of error shifts between different physical mechanisms.

The findings highlight the utility of mechanism-resolved diagnostics for understanding the underlying deficiencies of turbulence models. This approach offers valuable guidance for targeted improvements to RANS models, moving beyond simply matching overall engineering quantities to addressing specific physical modeling inaccuracies.
